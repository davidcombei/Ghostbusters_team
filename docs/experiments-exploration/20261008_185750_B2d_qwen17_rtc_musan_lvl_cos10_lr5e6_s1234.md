## 5. What went wrong / open problems (evidence)

### 5.1 Why 10 epochs did not help

- **The plateau is set by epoch 2-3 and is the same plateau.** Train acc crosses 95 % at epoch 2 in both runs (96.76 / 96.97 %) and the clean thr_oracle jumps 0.41 -> 0.99 at the same epoch; mini WF1@oracle at epoch 3 is 91.92 (B2d) vs 92.00 (B2c). Everything after that is +0.5-1.0 of drift, and the drift is in the ranking at the low-LR tail: B2d's best epochs are the three at LR <= 1.42e-6 (8, 9, 10), B2c's the two at LR <= 1.17e-6 (7, 8). Spending 2 more epochs at 1.9-4.8e-6 before that tail did not raise it (B2d 91.8-92.0 vs B2c 92.3-92.4 on half A; 91.84 vs 92.26 on half B).
- **The extra high-LR epochs added confidence, not separation.** At every aligned LR from 3.8e-6 down, B2d has equal train acc, higher dev loss (+0.03-0.04), higher noisy EER (+0.5-2.1) and lower noisy R_bona (1-4 points) than B2c. Final train loss 0.037 vs 0.042, thr_eer 1.000 / 1.000 / 0.999 (cl/ma/he) from epoch 9. The bona tail did not grow (16.4 vs 17.1 % of noisy bona at p >= 0.99, section 5.2), so the added confidence went into the spoof side and the dev-set bona (dev loss), i.e. into the already-saturated region, where WF1 cannot see it.
- **The dev-loss bump at epoch 4 is the one trajectory feature that differs**: 0.114 -> 0.154, with WF1 -0.80 and heldout-mini R_bona -6.1 in the same epoch, at LR 3.8-4.4e-6. B2c passed through the same LR window (epochs 2-3) with dev loss 0.081 -> 0.105 and no WF1 loss. Same seed, same data order; the only difference at that point is that B2d's LR was higher for longer (its epoch 3 ran at 4.36-4.76e-6 where B2c's ran at 3.95-4.58e-6). It reads as a transient over-fit phase that the shorter schedule did not have time for. It recovered on WF1 (epoch 8) but not on dev loss.
- **Why the merge lagged the final epoch again (-0.27)**: the merge contains epoch 3 (thr_or he 0.44, the early-plateau checkpoint; the same epoch 3 was in B2c's merge) and epoch 6 (dev loss 0.145); the three late epochs are within 0.23 of each other. The merge's oracle (92.36) is only 0.11 below the final epoch's (92.47) and its EER is better by 0.48, so once again the merge loses on the operating point, not the ranking.
